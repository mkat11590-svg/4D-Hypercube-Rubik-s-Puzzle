About the Puzzle
What is a Tesseract?
A tesseract (4D hypercube) is the 4-dimensional analog of a cube. Just as a cube has 6 square faces, a tesseract has 8 cubic cells. You're seeing a 3D perspective projection — like looking at a cube's shadow.
The 8 Cubic Cells
Each cell is a perfect 3D cube occupying one "face" of the 4D object. They're defined by fixing one of the four coordinates (X, Y, Z, or W) to either +1 or −1. Every vertex belongs to exactly 4 cells simultaneously.
4D Rotation Planes
In 4D space, rotation happens in a plane (not around an axis). Six unique planes exist. XY/XZ/YZ feel familiar. XW/YW/ZW involve the 4th dimension and look "impossible" — they exchange inner and outer regions of the projection.
